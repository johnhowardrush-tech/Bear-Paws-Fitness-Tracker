# Bear Paws Fitness Tracker - How to Use

**Site:** https://johnhowardrush-tech.github.io/Bear-Paws-Fitness-Tracker

A shared tracker where the six of us log workouts, weight, and blood pressure, and compete on a
leaderboard that's fair across different body sizes.

---

## First time: set up your profile

Do this before you log anything.

1. Open the site and go to **Profiles**
2. Find **your name** on the card header (cards are alphabetical: Howie, Jimmy, John, Michael, Tyler, Will)
3. Click **Edit stats**
4. Fill in:
   - **Height** in total inches (5'10" = 70)
   - **Starting weight** - your baseline; every number is measured against this
   - **Goal weight** - optional
   - **Starting blood pressure** - optional, but it's your BP baseline
5. Click **Save**

Double-check the name on the card before you edit. Your baseline drives your whole ranking.

---

## Every day: check in

Go to **Check In**:

- **Who you are** and the **date** (defaults to today)
- **Weight - required.** Every check-in needs it.
- **Blood pressure** - optional. Enter both numbers and it shows your category live.
- Worked out? Minutes and what you did
- Drinks
- Notes

Picking a person + date you've already logged loads those values back in, so re-saving **updates**
that day instead of creating a duplicate. You can fix past days freely.

## The five tabs

| Tab | What it's for |

| **Dashboard** | Who logged what today, top 3, full standings |

## Reading the numbers

**Every change is current minus start:**

- **Negative + green** = you went down. Good.
- **Positive + red** = you went up. Not good.

So if you started at 177 and weigh 178, you'll see **+1.0 lbs in red**. Started at 177 and weigh
172? **-5.0 lbs in green**.

### Weight: percent of body weight is the ranking metric

Raw pounds aren't fair across sizes:

- 10 lbs off 180 lbs = **-5.56%**
- 10 lbs off 220 lbs = **-4.55%**

Same ten pounds, but it's a bigger share of the smaller guy's body, so he ranks higher. Absolute
pounds and BMI change are shown too, but ranking is by percent.

### Blood pressure: we score it with MAP

**MAP (Mean Arterial Pressure)** = diastolic + (systolic - diastolic) / 3

One number instead of two, so 130/85 and 145/78 (both MAP 100) count the same. Healthy is roughly
70-100.

Categories follow the standard AHA bands:

| Category | Systolic | | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | under 120 | and | under 80 |
| Elevated | 120-129 | and | under 80 |
| Stage 1 | 130-139 | or | 80-89 |
| Stage 2 | 140+ | or | 90+ |
| Crisis | over 180 | or | over 120 |

**MAP change** is the BP version of percent-lost: how many MAP points you've dropped from your
baseline. That's the fair BP comparison and the thing to bet on - it rewards *improvement*, not
whoever got lucky with genetics. Someone going from MAP 105 to 95 (**-10**) beats someone who sat
at 88 the whole time (**0**).

Blood pressure is health data, not a diagnosis. If you're seeing Stage 2 or Crisis numbers, talk to
an actual doctor, not a leaderboard.

## Bets

- Everyone starts with **100 points**
- Pick who you're betting about, the metric (**weight** or **blood pressure**), and your call
  (they'll hit it / they'll miss it)
- Someone else can **take the other side** for the same stake
- When the deadline passes, hit **Resolve** - the winner takes the pot
- You can't bet on yourself, and you can't stake more points than you have

Example: *Howie bets 20 that John loses 5 lbs by Sunday.* Tyler takes the other side. Sunday comes,
John made it, so Howie takes Tyler's 20.

---

## Tips

- Weigh in at the same time of day - morning, before eating, is most consistent
- Log daily even on rest days
- BP once or twice a week is plenty
- Everyone sees everyone's data. There's no login, so don't enter anything you wouldn't say out loud.
